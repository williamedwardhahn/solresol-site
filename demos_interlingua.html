A thought you can see and hear

Every meaning is one point in a shared space, encoded as a short run of seven symbols — which are, at once, seven notes and seven colours. Pick a word. Watch it. Play it. Then fumble a note, and hear it land on a neighbour.

The first note became a meaning-family

Nobody assigned these. The code was built by clustering meaning — and the coarsest split, the first note, sorted itself into families. Animals share a first note; people share another; feelings another. Pick any word to load it.

Meaning is algebra you can play

The relationships live in the code, so analogies become operations on it. The result is lossy — it lands in the right family, not always the exact word — but it lands with intent.

How it works. A word → its meaning vector → a residual code over 7 symbols → a melody in the C-major octave and a sweep of the visible spectrum (one optical octave). A one-note slip decodes to a nearby meaning, not nonsense — the robustness that makes it safe to fumble.
